What the fitment factor is and why it matters

The fitment factor is the number your current basic pay is multiplied by to get your new basic pay when a pay commission is implemented. If your 7th CPC basic pay is ₹56,100 and the fitment factor is 1.92, your 8th CPC basic pay would be ₹56,100 × 1.92 = ₹1,07,712. Every allowance that is a percentage of basic pay (DA, HRA, transport DA, NPS) changes with it.

The 1.92 figure that is widely discussed comes from two parts: DA of about 60% being merged into basic pay (×1.60), and a real increase of about 20% on top (1.60 × 1.20 = 1.92). Staff federations have asked for higher factors, while some analysts expect lower ones. For comparison, the 6th CPC used 1.86 and the 7th CPC used 2.57.

The real hike is smaller than the factor suggests

A factor of 1.92 does not mean a 92% raise. Today you are paid basic pay plus 60% DA, so ₹18,000 basic is really ₹28,800. At 1.92 the new basic is ₹34,560 and DA starts again from 0%, so the increase is ₹5,760 a month, or 20%. At 2.28 the real increase is 42.5%, and at 2.86 it is 78.8%. The same percentages apply at every pay level. See the increase for your level on the 8th Pay Commission salary hike page.

When will the fitment factor be decided?

The 8th Pay Commission's Terms of Reference were notified in November 2025, and its report is due within about 18 months, so around mid-2027. The Government then decides the factor; the new pay is expected to count from 1 January 2026, with arrears for the months in between. Until then, every figure on this page is an estimate. For your own cell and in-hand salary, use the 8th CPC salary calculator or the full pay matrix.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fitment factor in the 8th Pay Commission?

It has not been announced yet. Estimates discussed range from about 1.83 to 2.86, with 1.92 the most widely quoted. The Commission will recommend a factor and the Government will decide.

How is the fitment factor calculated?

A common way is to merge the current DA into basic pay and add a real increase. With DA at about 60% and a 20% increase, the factor is 1.60 × 1.20 = 1.92.

What will the minimum salary be at a 1.92 fitment factor?

The minimum basic pay of ₹18,000 would become ₹34,560. At 2.28 it would be ₹41,040, and at 2.86 it would be ₹51,480.

What were the fitment factors in earlier pay commissions?

The 6th Pay Commission used 1.86 and the 7th Pay Commission used 2.57.

Is the 1.92 fitment factor confirmed?

No. 1.92 is the most widely quoted estimate, not an official figure. As of October 2026 the 8th Pay Commission has not announced a fitment factor; analyst estimates range from about 1.83 to 2.46 and employee unions have asked for 2.86 or more.

Does a 1.92 fitment factor mean a 92% salary hike?

No. DA of 60% merges into basic pay and restarts from 0%, so the real increase over today's basic + DA is 1.92 ÷ 1.60 − 1 = 20%. At 2.28 it is 42.5% and at 2.86 it is 78.8%.

When will the 8th Pay Commission fitment factor be announced?

The Commission's report is due within about 18 months of its Terms of Reference (November 2025), so around mid-2027. The Government decides the final factor after that.

📌 Official Calculation Methodology & Statutory Footnotes

Footer Notes: 7th & 8th CPC Rules, DoPT / DoE OMs, Pension & Tax Formulas

[Note 1] 8th CPC Fitment Factor Rule 7 CCS(RP)

Projected 8th CPC Basic Pay is computed as Round(7th CPC Basic × Fitment Factor). With Dearness Allowance reaching 60% on 01.01.2026, merging DA into Basic Pay yields a base factor of 1.60×; adding a 20% real pay hike produces the consensus 1.92× fitment factor (raising Level 1 minimum basic pay from ₹18,000 to ₹34,560).

[Note 2] DA Merger & AICPI-IW Jan & Jul Cycle

Dearness Allowance (DA) is revised every 1st January and 1st July based on the 12-month average of the All-India Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers (AICPI-IW, Base 2016=100) and applies to (Basic Pay + NPA). Upon 8th CPC implementation from 01.01.2026, existing DA merges into the new Basic Pay and resets to 0%.

[Note 3] HRA Slabs & Min Floor DoE OM 2/5/2017

Per DoE OM dated 07.07.2017, HRA is 30% (X), 20% (Y), and 10% (Z) when DA ≥ 50%, subject to a minimum floor of ₹5,400 (X), ₹3,600 (Y), and ₹1,800 (Z) in 7th CPC. When DA resets to 0% in 8th CPC, HRA percentage resets to 24% / 16% / 8% (revising to 27/18/9% at 25% DA and 30/20/10% at 50% DA), with minimum floors scaled proportionally.

[Note 4] Transport Allowance (TA) DoE OM 21/5/2017

Monthly TA is ₹1,350 / ₹900 for Level 1–2 (or ₹3,600 / ₹1,800 if 7th CPC Basic ≥ ₹24,200), ₹3,600 / ₹1,800 for Level 3–8, ₹7,200 / ₹3,600 for Level 9–13A, and ₹15,750 for Level 14+ (Higher TPTA 19 cities vs Other cities), plus DA on TA at the active DA rate. Divyangjan employees receive 2× normal TA.

[Note 5] NPA Ceiling, CGHS & CGEGIS MoHFW / DoE

Non-Practicing Allowance (NPA) for Central Health Service / Govt Doctors is 20% of Basic Pay subject to the statutory ceiling that Basic + NPA ≤ ₹2,37,500 in 7th CPC (scaled in 8th CPC). Mandatory monthly subscriptions: CGHS is ₹250 (L1–5), ₹450 (L6), ₹650 (L7–11), ₹1,000 (L12+); CGEGIS is ₹30 (Group C), ₹60 (Group B), ₹120 (Group A).

[Note 6] UPS, NPS & ₹25L Gratuity DoPPW 2025 Rules

Employee NPS/UPS Tier-I deduction is 10% of (Basic + NPA + DA). Under CCS (Pension) Rules 2021 (Rule 45) & DoPPW OM dated 18.06.2025, Central Govt employees under OPS and UPS qualify for Retirement/Death Gratuity at 1/4th of Emoluments per completed 6-month block (max 16.5×, capped at ₹25,00,000), plus UPS assured 50% pension (at 25+ yrs) and 1/10th lump-sum per 6-month block.

[Note 7] Income Tax FY 2026-27 IT Act 2025

New Tax Regime (Tax Year 2026-27) provides a ₹75,000 Standard Deduction, Employer 14% NPS deduction u/s 80CCD(2), and Section 87A tax rebate up to ₹60,000 (making net taxable income up to ₹12,00,000 / gross salary up to ₹12.75L+ completely tax-free, with marginal relief above ₹12L) plus 4% Health & Education Cess.

[Note 8] Arrears & Form 10E Relief Sec 89(1) Relief

Based on the 10-year Pay Commission cycle, the 8th CPC effective date is 01.01.2026. In 6th & 7th CPC precedents, arrears were paid on revised Basic + DA (less 10% NPS), whereas revised HRA/TA took effect from the date of notification. Lump-sum arrears qualify for retrospective slab relief under Section 89(1) (Form 10E).
