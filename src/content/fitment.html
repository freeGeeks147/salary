What the fitment factor is and why it matters

The fitment factor is the number your current basic pay is multiplied by to get your new basic pay when a pay commission is implemented. If your 7th CPC basic pay is ₹56,100 and the fitment factor is 1.92, your 8th CPC basic pay would be ₹56,100 × 1.92 = ₹1,07,712. Every allowance that is a percentage of basic pay (DA, HRA, transport DA, NPS) changes with it.

The 1.92 figure that is widely discussed comes from two parts: DA of about 60% being merged into basic pay (×1.60), and a real increase of about 20% on top (1.60 × 1.20 = 1.92). DA has since risen to 63% (from July 2026), so the same 20% increase would now give about 1.96. Staff federations have asked for higher factors, while some analysts expect lower ones. For comparison, the 6th CPC used 1.86 and the 7th CPC used 2.57.

The real hike is smaller than the factor suggests

A factor of 1.92 does not mean a 92% raise. Today you are paid basic pay plus 63% DA, so ₹18,000 basic is really ₹29,340. At 1.92 the new basic is ₹34,560 and DA starts again from 0%, so the increase is ₹5,220 a month, or about 18%. At 2.28 the real increase is about 40%, and at 2.86 about 75%. The same percentages apply at every pay level. See the increase for your level on the 8th Pay Commission salary hike page.

When will the fitment factor be decided?

The 8th Pay Commission was constituted on 3 November 2025 and must submit its report within 18 months, so by about May 2027. The Government then decides the factor. The effective date is not officially fixed, but the new pay is widely expected to count from 1 January 2026, with arrears for the months in between. Follow the 8th Pay Commission latest news for updates. Until then, every figure on this page is an estimate.
