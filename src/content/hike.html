How the 8th Pay Commission hike is worked out

When the 8th Pay Commission is implemented, your basic pay is multiplied by the fitment factor, and the DA you get today (60% of basic pay) is merged into it and starts again from 0%. So the fair comparison is the new basic pay against today's basic pay plus DA. That is why the hike at the widely quoted factor of 1.92 is 20%, not 92%.

For example, at Level 1 you get ₹18,000 basic + ₹10,800 DA = ₹28,800 today. At 1.92 the new basic is ₹34,560: ₹5,760 more a month, or ₹69,120 a year. At Level 10 (₹56,100 basic), the increase at 1.92 is ₹17,952 a month. Because every basic pay is multiplied by the same factor, the percentage hike is the same at every level; the rupee amount grows with your pay.

What else changes in your in-hand salary

HRA and transport allowance are linked to basic pay, so they rise too, but HRA rates are expected to drop back to 24% / 16% / 8% once DA restarts from 0%. NPS contributions and income tax rise with pay as well. For your exact cell, city and deductions, open the 8th CPC salary calculator (each "In-hand" link in the table opens it with that level filled in). The new pay is expected to count from 1 January 2026, so the gap until it is paid will come as arrears: estimate yours with the arrears calculator.
